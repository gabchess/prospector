## Assessment at 20 September

The operations manager is a problem owner and possible internal sponsor. IT owns a review requirement; spending authority and implementation ownership remain unknown. A scheduled evaluation establishes a next conversation, not purchase approval.

Current posture: **validate**. Confidence is limited by unknown authority and budget. The leading explanation is an active evaluation with unresolved approval conditions. An alternative is exploratory interest without a funded project. A named approval owner and decision process would distinguish them.

Next move: the seller asks the manager who owns spending approval and invites IT to define its review requirements. Prerequisite: the user approves the message. Success signal: named owners and a dated review step. Disconfirming result: no owner or project window. Avoid pricing concessions, fabricated ROI and a proposal that assumes approval. Review after the evaluation call; hold if no decision window exists.

Draft: “Before we prepare a proposal, could you confirm who owns budget approval and what IT needs to review? We can use the evaluation call to agree on those steps.” This is a draft, not a sent message.

Update: 22 Sep. New E6: fictional IT note says access review cannot start until the buyer names an implementation owner. E5 remains true but incomplete. Posture changes from validate to hold pending that owner. Preserve the earlier assessment. Re-enter when the manager supplies the owner and review date; do not add follow-up volume to an ownerless process.
