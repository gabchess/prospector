# Opportunity review

Use this for a user-presented active opportunity or an owner-accepted research handoff. The output is a useful decision and a case that another session can continue.

## Recover the case

Read the existing case before new analysis. For a new case, adapt [the case template](../assets/opportunity.md) to the user-approved private workspace. Ask for the offer, buyer change, current objective and available evidence; begin a provisional read with what exists. Leave unknown fields visible. Do not require invented stakeholders or a complete template before helping.

Give consequential claims stable IDs. Record the source and date, with one evidence state:

- observed: present in a source;
- buyer-stated: attributed to a buyer, without assuming independent confirmation;
- inferred or assumed: interpretation or working premise, with the basis and a way to test it;
- unknown: missing;
- disputed: sources conflict;
- disconfirmed: later evidence defeats an earlier claim.

Missing support is not disconfirmation. Keep authority unknown when it was inferred only from a title; preserve the old assertion as an unsupported inference. Use disconfirmed only when contrary evidence defeats it. Separate the observed existence of a note from whether its claim is true.

Retain superseded claims and link corrections to their IDs. A new message may change the assessment without invalidating every prior observation.

## Understand the decision

Identify the business change the buyer would accept, who must evaluate it, who can approve spending, and who must implement it. Map security, procurement, finance or legal involvement only when relevant. For each role, separate confirmed authority from a title or inferred influence. Keep missing owners visible.

Assess a possible champion through observed actions: access to other decision-makers, internal coordination, sharing decision criteria and willingness to own a next step. Friendly language is insufficient. Do not infer private motives or a person's vulnerabilities.

Check whether activity changed the buying conditions. A booked meeting, successful pilot or procurement questionnaire can coexist with missing budget, no implementation owner or an unresolved veto. Compare the leading explanation with at least one credible alternative when the diagnosis changes the recommendation. State what evidence would distinguish them.

Review the cost of proceeding: buyer effort, implementation burden, commercial fit, unresolved dependencies and the user's time. Never invent expected revenue, probability of closing or legal/security acceptance.

## Recommend a move

Choose one current posture from the evidence:

| Posture | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| Advance | The prerequisites for a concrete buyer commitment are supported. |
| Validate | A decisive unknown or contradiction needs a targeted check. |
| Recover | A previously viable path has stalled and has a bounded repair. |
| Hold | A named event or date governs re-entry. |
| Disqualify | The opportunity fails a known fit condition. |
| Exit | Further investment has no supported path or exceeds the owner's limits. |

For the recommended move record: governing condition, supporting claim IDs, prerequisites, action owner, expected response, success signal, disconfirming result, actions to avoid and a review date or event. State confidence in plain language with its limiting evidence. Choose a smaller move if prerequisites are absent.

Hold and exit remain valid outcomes. Do not substitute more follow-up activity for a missing decision path. On a closed, lost or transferred case, preserve the outcome evidence and the receiving owner; re-entry requires a new event and scope.

## Draft after the diagnosis

When asked for a message, establish its recipient, internal carrier, purpose and exposure. Write a short forwardable draft using supported claims and the next agreed question or commitment. Keep private strategy notes separate from the message. Surface open conditions instead of promising unapproved ROI, delivery dates, compliance or commercial terms. Never present a draft as sent.

## Update and recover

Append each material update: new evidence, corrected claims, prior-to-current assessment, stakeholder changes, intervention result, revised posture and the next review event. Keep the previous file version or a dated snapshot before replacement; use one writer at a time. If two versions differ, reconcile them explicitly before continuing.

If tools fail, stop external actions and retain the last confirmed case. Provide a portable text update when writes are unavailable. At resumption, read the case and check source dates rather than reconstructing it from chat memory. A stale statement is not a fresh buying condition.
